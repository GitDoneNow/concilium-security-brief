Welcome to the Concilium Security Risk Brief for Thursday, November nineteenth. Five minutes of security coverage from the last twenty four hours, to increase your situational awareness without decreasing your productivity. Here's what matters today.

Today: deadly unrest and a shutdown in Bangladesh, a Houthi missile threat that caused damage in Riyadh, a new U.S. security alert for Bahrain, U.S. sanctions on a charity accused of fronting for Hezbollah, and a standoff in Iraq over Jurf al Sakhr.

We start in Bangladesh. Reuters reports parts of the country shut down today, as police said two ruling party members were killed and the ancestral home of former leader Muhammad Yunus was set ablaze, in violence ahead of former Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina's return, planned for December. Reuters says Hasina's supporters are escalating protests. The Times of India reports the Yunus family home is in Chattogram, and that crude bomb explosions were reported as violence intensified across the country.

Next, Saudi Arabia. Reuters reports the Saudi led coalition said a Houthi missile threat toward Riyadh caused damage, after a loud boom was heard in the capital overnight. Al Arabiya reports the coalition also said it intercepted and destroyed five Houthi ballistic missiles and a cruise missile launched toward southern Saudi Arabia, and destroyed a ballistic missile launch platform in Dhamar, Yemen. The State Department urged U.S. citizens to review its travel advisory for Saudi Arabia, and said Americans in the Middle East should exercise heightened vigilance.

Turning to Bahrain. The State Department says Americans in Bahrain should exercise heightened vigilance, due to the increased threat of armed attacks by Iranian supported Houthis and Iranian efforts to target U.S. government officials and facilities in the region. It advises being aware of potential alerts and flight cancellations.

On sanctions. The Treasury Department's Office of Foreign Assets Control designated the Wa Ta'awanu Association, a charity it says provides cover for Hezbollah's activities under the guise of delivering humanitarian services. Treasury also designated members of a gold smuggling network based in Lebanon and Türkiye. The State Department spokesperson said Hezbollah's exploitation of humanitarian groups and private organizations is shameful.

And in Iraq, Critical Threats assesses that Prime Minister Ali al Zaydi likely aimed to reach an agreement with Quds Force commander Esmail Ghaani to force the Iranian backed militia Kataib Hezbollah to withdraw from Jurf al Sakhr, south of Baghdad, while avoiding armed conflict between the government and the militia.

Now a few quick notes by region.

In the Middle East, an update on Iran. Radio Free Europe reports hundreds of people protested after thirteen oil and gas workers died in a bus crash in the western city of Abdanan, and the Fars news agency said the crowds grew to about one thousand. The Institute for the Study of War assesses the regime may respond with further nationwide security deployments.

In Europe, Le Figaro reports that a building housing the regional health agency in Bastia, on the French island of Corsica, was targeted with an explosive or incendiary device overnight from Monday, November sixteenth. France's national anti terrorism prosecutor's office has taken the case, and Le Figaro says it is the first action attributed to the underground group F L N C U C, which is trying to resume armed struggle, since a widely noted press conference.

On cyber threats, The Record reports Norwegian energy company Equinor is working to contain a cyberattack on its I T systems, and the company says oil and gas production has not been disrupted. Reuters reports the top U.S. intelligence official said disruptions to critical systems over the past week were not a coordinated or state sponsored attack.

In Africa, ACLED says violence involving Boko Haram has declined in Nigeria's Borno State since a reported ceasefire in June, but the group has intensified operations in Cameroon and Chad, where attacks on civilians have surged, particularly kidnappings for ransom.
