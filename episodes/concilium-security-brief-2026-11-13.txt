Welcome to the Concilium Security Risk Brief for Friday, November thirteenth. Five minutes of security coverage from the last twenty four hours, to increase your situational awareness without decreasing your productivity. Here's what matters today.

Today: Iran says it targeted a U.S. radar in Bahrain, new Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia and fresh U.N. sanctions on the group, a rising death toll in eastern Congo, a deadly drone attack on Volgograd in Russia, and a grenade attack on a broadcaster in Cali, Colombia.

We start with Iran and Bahrain. Reuters reports Iran's Revolutionary Guards said they targeted a U.S. early warning radar in Bahrain, the first such claim of an attack on American military assets in a Gulf country since early September. Al Jazeera reports the Guards called it retaliation for an attack on a telecommunications center in Iran. Arab News, citing Iran's state news agency, reports the southern port city of Sirik was hit by what Iranian officials called an enemy strike yesterday.

Next, Saudi Arabia and Yemen. Houthi spokesmen claimed attacks on a military cargo plane at Sharurah airport, the Khamis Mushait air base and Aramco facilities, according to regional outlets including South24. Arab News reports the Saudi led coalition said it intercepted and destroyed two drones in the Riyadh region and another launched toward the Eastern Province. Arab News also reports the U.N. Security Council renewed its sanctions on Yemen and moved to cut off dual use components and chemicals the Houthis use to build drones and missiles. And the Institute for the Study of War says the Houthis launched an offensive in southeastern Lahij province on Wednesday, November eleventh, that likely aims to cut the road between Mokha and Aden.

Turning to the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Radio Okapi reports at least thirty five people were killed and fourteen are missing after attacks attributed to the A D F rebels in Mambasa territory, Ituri province. Local outlets had first put the toll at twenty two on Wednesday.

In Russia, Reuters reports four people were killed and two injured in a Ukrainian drone attack on an industrial zone in Volgograd, the deadliest attack on the city so far, regional governor Andrei Bocharov said. Separately, Reuters reports a Russian serviceman was killed when the car he was driving was blown up in St. Petersburg, investigators said.

And in Colombia. On Wednesday, November eleventh, a grenade was thrown at the headquarters of broadcaster R C N in Cali, damaging the building, local media including R T C C Noticias reported. Colombia's press freedom foundation condemned the attack.

Now a few quick notes by region.

In South Asia, Dawn reports Balochistan police said two civilians were killed and three paramilitary personnel and thirteen civilians injured as security forces repelled an attack on the Kandawa post in Mastung. Dawn also reports Pakistan's military said thirty five militants were killed in Mir Ali, North Waziristan, and eight in Orakzai.

In the Middle East, the Institute for the Study of War says Iran continues to claim that commercial vessels are striking naval mines along the U.S. backed route in the southern Strait of Hormuz, likely to undermine confidence in U.S. escort operations.

In Europe, an update on the school bomb threats: The Record reports the threats sent to thousands of schools across Europe this week came from a compromised email account at a Taiwanese university. And Reuters reports Norway's security service says Russian intelligence could carry out sabotage in the coming year.

In Africa, the International Committee of the Red Cross says it provided assistance to seventy two hundred people displaced by renewed hostilities in Ethiopia's Tigray region.

And in cyber, Reuters reports the F B I says North Korean hackers stole more than four hundred million dollars using video images. The Record reports suspected Kazakhstan linked hackers used an artificial intelligence agent to help spy on Central Asian governments, according to new research.
