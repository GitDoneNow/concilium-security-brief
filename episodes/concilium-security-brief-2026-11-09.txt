Welcome to the Concilium Security Risk Brief for Monday, November ninth. Five minutes of security coverage from the last twenty four hours, to increase your situational awareness without decreasing your productivity. Here's what matters today.

Today: a deadly arson attack on a family in northwest Nigeria, deadly airstrikes near Yemen's capital and a new alert in Riyadh, a drone attack that cut gas and power across Iraq's Kurdistan region, a migrant boat disaster off Senegal, and police abducted in an attack in northwest Pakistan.

We start with Nigeria. Reuters reports that gunmen attacked a government backed vigilante in Gidan Taba village in Zamfara State's Maradun area on Saturday night, November seventh. Police said the attackers killed him and ten relatives, set their home on fire and abducted thirteen people. The local government chairman told Daily Trust that twelve family members died after they were trapped in a burning room. Police said security forces have been deployed to secure the hostages' release.

Next, Yemen and Saudi Arabia. The Associated Press reports that airstrikes on a village near Sanaa on Saturday, November seventh, killed at least twenty seven people and wounded thirty six, according to the Houthi run health ministry, which blamed Saudi Arabia. Al Jazeera reports Saudi Arabia had not commented. The Houthis said their attacks on Saudi Arabia were in response. Reuters reports Saudi civil defense raised an alert in Riyadh earlier today, then said minutes later that the danger had passed.

Turning to Iraq. Reuters reports production was halted at the Khor Mor gas field in the Kurdistan region after a suspected drone attack late Sunday. The operator, Dana Gas, said the fire was put out, no staff were hurt, and it is assessing the damage. The regional electricity ministry said about two point eight gigawatts of power generation was lost, cutting gas to power plants serving Erbil, Sulaimani and Duhok. Rudaw reports Iraq's prime minister ordered a joint investigation.

In Senegal. BBC Africa reports at least thirty four people died when a boat carrying migrants toward Spain's Canary Islands sank off the northwest coast, citing the Senegalese Press Agency. Media reports say the boat capsized on Saturday, November seventh, though one migrant aid group says it sank on Friday. Emergency services later told A F P the toll was at least thirty five, with one survivor found and an unknown number missing.

And in Pakistan. The Express Tribune reports militants attacked a police post in Hangu district in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa late Sunday, killing one constable and abducting three others, according to the district police chief. Police said they regained control of the post and launched a pursuit operation.

Now a few quick notes by region.

In Europe, Reuters reports the gunman who killed six people in Krakow's main square on Saturday has died in hospital, police said. Radio Free Europe reports a Ukrainian drone attack sparked a fire at Russia's Kirishi oil refinery near Saint Petersburg today, and the regional governor said a woman was killed by falling debris. The Institute for the Study of War says trilateral negotiations involving Ukrainian and Russian delegations are scheduled for November tenth and eleventh in Abu Dhabi.

In the Middle East, Reuters reports President Trump said he is highly unlikely to order fresh strikes on Iran as long as talks progress, even as both countries reported shipping hit in or near the Strait of Hormuz.

In Africa, BBC Africa reports at least seven people were killed and more than one hundred injured when two passenger trains collided near Giza in Egypt on Sunday. And Australia's Smartraveller says Mauritius has introduced Ebola border measures, including health checks on arrival and entry restrictions for travelers recently in the Democratic Republic of the Congo or South Sudan.

In South Asia, Dawn reports water surged into work areas of the Tarbela Dam extension project in Pakistan after a rock mass collapsed during excavation. The water authority said all workers were safely evacuated and no one was hurt.

In the Asia Pacific, the U.S. Geological Survey reports a preliminary magnitude six point four earthquake southwest of Lata in the Solomon Islands. Reuters reports a magnitude five point seven earthquake in central Japan on Sunday injured at least twenty two people. And Smartraveller says aircraft with more than thirty passenger seats are barred from Santo International Airport in Vanuatu until firefighting services are fully operational.

And in cyber, Reuters reports Italy says Group of Seven ministers will discuss recent cyber attacks.
