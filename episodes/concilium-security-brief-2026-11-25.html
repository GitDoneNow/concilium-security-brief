Welcome to the Concilium Security Risk Brief for Wednesday, November twenty fifth. Five minutes of security coverage from the last twenty four hours, to increase your situational awareness without decreasing your productivity. Here's what matters today.

Today: an evacuation warning for Yemen's Hodeidah port and a Houthi threat against Saudi Red Sea ports, a hostage standoff in Cartagena, Colombia, drone strikes near Iranian Kurdish opposition camps in northern Iraq, a missing military aircraft in eastern Congo, and new Ebola screening rules for travelers to Malawi.

We start in Yemen. Reuters reports the Saudi-led coalition issued an evacuation warning for Yemen's Hodeidah port. Reuters also reports the Houthis said the Saudi port of Jeddah and all other Saudi ports on the Red Sea coast would be targeted if Saudi Arabia carried out threats to attack Hodeidah.

Next, Colombia. Reuters reports about twenty people were held hostage in a supermarket in Cartagena yesterday after a failed armored car hijacking. Colombian media, including El Colombiano and El Universo, report security forces rescued all of the hostages alive after more than fifteen hours, and that one of the hostage takers was killed in the operation. President Abelardo de la Espriella praised the operation.

Turning to northern Iraq. Rudaw English reports three one way attack drones struck near two camps of the Kurdistan Democratic Party of Iran in Erbil province yesterday evening, including the Azadi camp in Koya, according to a party official and a security source. No casualties were reported.

In Congo, BBC Africa reports the government is searching for one of its aircraft after rebels said they shot down a warplane in the east. Local reports say the army lost contact with a Cessna cargo plane over South Kivu on Sunday, November twenty second, near Minembwe.

And in Malawi, the U.K. Foreign Office updated its travel advice with Malawi's Ebola screening measures. Travelers arriving from or transiting through Uganda, Kenya or Nigeria will be monitored daily for twenty one days, and travelers arriving from Congo must self isolate for twenty one days under daily monitoring.

Now a few quick notes by region.

In Africa, ACLED says Ethiopian forces regained control of two locations near Badme on Monday, November twenty third, with Eritrean forces reportedly reducing their presence in the area. In Somalia, the Somali Guardian reports al-Shabaab released images of a destroyed armored vehicle it says belonged to a Ugandan military convoy it attacked in the Lower Shabelle region.

In Europe, Russia's Federal Security Service says it foiled a planned attack on a synagogue in Kislovodsk, in southern Russia, according to i24NEWS.

In Asia, Reuters reports North Korea expelled a Swiss diplomat over sanctions, weeks after the Swiss office reopened.

On sanctions and terrorism listings, the George Washington University Program on Extremism reports Canada on Monday, November twenty third, added the group Masar Badil to its terrorist entity list as an alias of Samidoun.

On cyber, The Record reports Britain's National Cyber Security Centre has observed a marked rise since September in cyberattacks assisted by artificial intelligence. The Record also reports Spain's professional soccer league, LaLiga, said hackers stole fans' personal information in a cyberattack.

In the Middle East, the U.N. humanitarian office says some three hundred fifty thousand children in Taiz, Yemen, missed a national measles and rubella vaccination campaign because insecurity prevented vaccine delivery.
