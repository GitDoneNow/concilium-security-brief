Security Risk Brief: Sunday, November 1, 2026
Concilium Security Risk Brief

Welcome to the Concilium Security Risk Brief for Sunday, November first. Five minutes of security coverage from the last twenty four hours, to increase your situational awareness without decreasing your productivity. Here's what matters today.

Today: a threat warning in Moscow, flooding in the Galápagos, Ebola testing in Nigeria, violence in India's Manipur state, and fighting near Aden in Yemen.

We start in Russia. The U.S. State Department says the U.S. Embassy in Moscow was aware of a credible threat of a mass casualty event yesterday at the Tsaritsyno Museum Reserve in Moscow, and that the U.S. government was coordinating with Russian authorities on a response. Canada's travel advisory service relayed the alert. The independent Russian outlet Meduza reports Russia's F S B security service said it received a U.S. warning about a terror plot in Moscow.

Next, Ecuador. The U.S. State Department says heavy rains flooded homes on Santa Cruz Island in the Galápagos, and that local authorities declared an emergency on Friday, October thirtieth. It says a house collapsed, power is out in the island's highlands, and the road between Puerto Ayora and Bellavista is closed. The State Department also says Baltra airport has temporarily suspended operations. Canada and the U.K. Foreign Office both updated their Ecuador advice for the flooding.

Turning to Nigeria, and an Ebola update. Nigeria's Centre for Disease Control said two suspected Ebola cases were identified in the northeast and isolated. It later said samples from the two people, in Yola, Adamawa State, tested negative after two rounds of testing. Separately, BBC Africa reports the Ebola treatment centre in Mongbwalu, in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, has discharged its last patients with confirmed Ebola.

Next, India. Reuters reports five people were killed in fresh ethnic violence in Manipur state. The New Indian Express reports police said three Kuki men were killed in a gunfight between rival armed groups in Noney district yesterday.

And in Yemen. The Institute for the Study of War assesses that recent Houthi attempts to advance from Taiz governorate into Lahij governorate may indicate the Houthis are trying to set conditions to besiege or capture the city of Aden. It says government-aligned forces reportedly recaptured some areas in Lahij.

Now a few quick notes by region.

In the Middle East, the Institute for the Study of War and the Critical Threats Project report Iran's Supreme Leader, Mojtaba Khamenei, directed Iran's three top military commanders to prepare to retaliate against any U.S. strikes by expanding Iran's list of targets. Reuters reports Iranian envoys met Qatari mediators as the threat of U.S. strikes after the midterm elections looms.

In Africa, Libya's National Oil Corporation said an armed group forcibly shut a valve on the main crude oil pipeline linking the Hamada field to the Zawiya refinery, on Friday, October thirtieth, according to local and regional media.

And in Europe, an update on the evacuation from Oleshky in southern Ukraine. Radio Free Europe reports hundreds of residents have now arrived in Ukrainian-controlled territory, helped by a convoy of twenty five buses. Reuters reports four planes carrying evacuated residents landed in Belarus.
