Concilium Security Risk Brief

Welcome to the Concilium Security Risk Brief for Friday, November twenty seventh. Five minutes of security coverage from the last twenty four hours, to increase your situational awareness without decreasing your productivity. Here's what matters today.

Today: two Italian workers killed in a drone attack in Kuwait, a deadly strike on a Saudi refinery in Jazan, a deadly night raid in Nigeria's Plateau State, arrests in Germany over a synagogue bomb plot, and new Australian sanctions over Iran-directed arson attacks.

We start in Kuwait. Reuters reports two Italian workers were killed in an attack on a site in Kuwait yesterday, with a source saying it was an Iranian strike. Al Jazeera reports Kuwait confirmed the deaths, and the Italian news agency ANSA says the two worked for the German defense company Rheinmetall. Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, rejected claims that Tehran was behind the attack and called the incident suspicious.

Turning to Saudi Arabia. Reuters reports an attack on Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery killed a worker and halted some operations yesterday. Al Jazeera, citing Saudi Arabia's energy ministry, says the worker was a Nepali national and that others were injured, and the Saudi-led coalition said the Houthis carried out the attack.

Next, Nigeria. TheCable and THISDAY report gunmen killed at least sixteen people in an overnight attack on the Tafi Gana community near Miango, in Bassa Local Government Area of Plateau State, on Wednesday night. Local media report the state government put the toll at seven killed and three injured, and that the governor directed security agencies to respond. The Tracking Terrorism and Counter Extremism group attributes the attack to Fulani herdsmen.

In Germany, federal prosecutors say police arrested two Iraqi nationals in Heidelberg and Cologne yesterday on suspicion of membership in a pro-Iranian terrorist group. Prosecutors say the group recruited people for bomb attacks, and that in late March unknown suspects, presumably carrying explosives, went to synagogues in Cologne and another European city, but no attack was carried out. Deutsche Welle reports more than two hundred fifty officers took part in searches at seventeen locations.

And in Australia, Reuters reports the government placed counterterrorism financing sanctions on two people it said orchestrated antisemitic arson attacks in Sydney and Melbourne on behalf of Iran.

Now a few quick notes by region.

In Europe, Australia's Smartraveller says Estonia will close its border crossings with Russia from December first. Reuters reports a drone briefly violated Latvian airspace, according to Latvia's armed forces. Off Ibiza, Reuters reports a crew member died after a diesel tanker capsized, and Spanish authorities are working to contain any spill.

In Africa, Canada's travel advisory service says the Gambia holds general elections on December fifth and demonstrations could occur before, during and after the vote. The U.K. Foreign Office updated its Mauritius advice with information about local demonstrations. In Egypt, Al Jazeera reports a fuel tanker overturned and caught fire on a Cairo highway, killing thirty one people, and prosecutors detained the driver. And in an update on Ebola, Reuters reports a Kenyan court ruled the government breached the constitution by allowing a U.S.-backed Ebola quarantine site at a military air base.

In the Middle East, Al Jazeera reports Iran's army navy commander said Iranian forces recovered two Iranian oil tankers he said had been seized by the U.S. military, according to Iranian state media. The U.S. has not commented. The I C R C says it facilitated the transfer of human remains across front lines in Yemen from November twenty fourth to twenty sixth.

In Asia, T R T World reports a crude bomb exploded outside a court in Dhaka, Bangladesh, injuring a lawyer, and police detained a suspect.

In Latin America, Reuters reports, citing sources, that a fire prompted the evacuation of workers at Venezuela's Amuay refinery.

In the Pacific, Smartraveller says Vanuatu's health ministry declared an influenza A outbreak in Port Vila and on Efate Island.

On cyber, Reuters reports Standard Chartered's crypto unit, Zodia Custody, was hit by a security breach that exposed some client data. Reuters also reports India's Election Commission fixed a website flaw that exposed some voters' personal details, after Reuters alerted it.
