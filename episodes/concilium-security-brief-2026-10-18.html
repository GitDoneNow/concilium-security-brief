Welcome to the Concilium Security Risk Brief for Sunday, October eighteenth. Five minutes of security coverage from the last twenty four hours, to increase your situational awareness without decreasing your productivity. Here's what matters today.

We start with Saudi Arabia and Yemen. The State Department travel account reminds US citizens in Saudi Arabia to follow national alerts from Saudi Civil Defense sent by cellular broadcast. It also says US government employees in the country now need special authorization from the Chief of Mission to use any Saudi airport. The UK Foreign Office updated its Saudi Arabia safety and security advice to reflect the latest regional tensions. In Yemen, the Institute for the Study of War says government-aligned forces appear to have repelled Houthi efforts to take positions on the western outskirts of Taiz City, and that the Houthis targeted government weapons depots near Aden International Airport. ACLED says the Houthis have encircled Taiz, while government forces are making some gains in Marib. The Institute adds that government forces must hold ground in Waziyah and Madaribah districts to protect the recently recaptured Dhubab Airport from Houthi attempts to outflank them.

In the Strait of Hormuz, Radio Free Europe reports Iran's Revolutionary Guards said a supertanker carrying more than two million barrels of crude oil exploded and caught fire after hitting a naval mine. The Institute for the Study of War notes an uptick in attacks on vessels near the strait.

On the Pakistan and Afghanistan border, the Pakistani newspaper Dawn, citing state television, reports the army responded to what it called unprovoked firing with heavy weapons, destroying several Afghan Taliban posts. Global Decipher reports the clashes began in the Upper Kurram area of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and spread to border sectors in North and South Waziristan, with casualties reported on both sides.

In Kenya, BBC Africa reports four new cases of Bundibugyo Ebola, bringing the total to seven. Three of the new patients are health workers who treated the country's first case, and the fourth is a relative. These are the first known infections passed on inside Kenya. The health ministry says there is no evidence of community transmission and the risk to the public is low.

In Ukraine, Radio Free Europe reports Russia launched a major overnight barrage of ballistic and cruise missiles and more than three hundred drones, killing civilians, damaging apartment buildings and striking energy infrastructure. Officials in both Ukraine and Russia also reported civilian deaths after further drone attacks overnight into Sunday. Separately, Radio Free Europe and the Institute for the Study of War report President Trump signed an executive order on the night of October sixteenth waiving nearly all substantive sanctions, tariffs and restrictions in a bipartisan law aimed at pressuring Russia to end the war. The Institute assesses the decision will help Russia finance and prolong the war.

Now a few quick notes by region.

In the Middle East and North Africa, the International Committee of the Red Cross supported the release of one hundred fifty nine people detained in connection with the conflict in Yemen. Long War Journal reports Lebanon revoked an Iranian bank's license amid US pressure over Hezbollah financing.

In Africa, BBC Africa reports a pastor and his wife were arrested in South Africa after two hundred people, some in chains, were found held at a suspected illegal rehabilitation centre.

In South Asia, Dawn reports Pakistani security forces say they killed five militants who tried to set up a roadblock near Quetta, in Balochistan.

In Asia Pacific, Canada raised its advice for Guam to avoid non-essential travel because of Typhoon Champi.

In Europe, the Institute for the Study of War reports Polish authorities warn that Russia may escalate sabotage attempts against Polish logistics infrastructure.
