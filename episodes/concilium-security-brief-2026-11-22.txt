Welcome to the Concilium Security Risk Brief for Sunday, November twenty second. Five minutes of security coverage from the last twenty four hours, to increase your situational awareness without decreasing your productivity. Here's what matters today.

Today: a new Iranian attack on Bahrain, cartel violence in Mexico's Sinaloa state after a major arrest, a drone strike on a Russian cargo ship in the Gulf of Finland, and a quadcopter attack on a peace meeting in northwest Pakistan.

We start in Bahrain. Reuters reports Bahrain says an Iranian attack hit an industrial facility as its air defenses intercepted attacks. Separately, Reuters reports the United States denied an Iranian account that a missile damaged a U.S. F-16 over the Gulf.

Next, Mexico. The State Department says the U.S. Consulate General in Hermosillo is monitoring reports of security operations, armed clashes, road blockades and vehicle fires overnight in Ahome, Guamúchil, Guasave and Navolato in Sinaloa state, following the arrest of Fausto Isidro Meza Flores. Crisis Group says the cartel leader, known as El Chapo Isidro, was captured by Mexican authorities in Guasave on Friday night, November twentieth.

Turning to the Baltic. The Institute for the Study of War says two Ukrainian drones struck the Russian cargo ship Sparta Two, which has supported Russian military logistics, in the Gulf of Finland. Reuters reports Finland says the ship is on fire in its waters, that Finland has tightened security, and that the ship was still burning early today.

And in Pakistan, Tribal News Network reports a quadcopter attack on a jirga, or council of elders, in Bannu, in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province, injured ten people, including children, with several in critical condition. Global Decipher reports at least twenty were injured. Accounts differ on who carried out the attack.

Now a few quick notes by region.

In the Middle East, the Institute for the Study of War says the Houthis are reportedly reinforcing positions and military capabilities along the Bab el Mandeb Strait, citing unnamed military and local sources, and assesses that Saudi Arabia is carrying out airstrikes on Houthi energy infrastructure. In Iran, Radio Free Europe Radio Liberty reports the government said it will ration electricity, days after it began managing bread purchases because of shortages.

In Africa, on Thursday night, November nineteenth, gunmen attacked the edge of Nangade town in Mozambique's Cabo Delgado province. Zitamar News reports six people were killed. The terrorism monitor TRAC says Christians were targeted and attributes the attack to Islamic State Mozambique.

In Europe, the Institute for the Study of War says Ukraine's president proposed temporary local ceasefires to evacuate civilians from fifteen settlements along the Dnipro River in occupied Kherson and Zaporizhzhia regions, and that Russian forces recently advanced in several areas of eastern Ukraine.

In the Americas, InSight Crime reports violence is rising in a Venezuelan mining zone disputed by the E L N and the Second Marquetalia, displacing Indigenous communities.
