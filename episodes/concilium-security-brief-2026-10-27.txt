Welcome to the Concilium Security Risk Brief for Tuesday, October twenty seventh. Five minutes of security coverage from the last twenty four hours, to increase your situational awareness without decreasing your productivity. Here's what matters today.

We start in Ethiopia. BBC Africa and Reuters report that more than sixty people were killed in attacks on Christians in the Oromia region over the past week, according to the Ethiopian Orthodox Church. A local official blamed a new rebel coalition that includes the Oromo Liberation Army, which has not commented and has denied carrying out previous such attacks. Separately, the U.K. Foreign Office updated its Ethiopia travel advice yesterday with new information about increased tensions and armed groups.

In the Middle East, Reuters reports Jordan carried out airstrikes in southern Syria after linking the attackers in the recent attack on a church in Jordan to terrorist groups. Dawn, citing health officials in Syria's Daraa province, reports six people were killed, including three children and a woman. Syria's border and customs authority then closed the Nassib border crossing with Jordan, suspending passenger and commercial traffic in both directions from today, according to its statement carried by C G T N Europe.

In Saudi Arabia, Reuters reports the Saudi led coalition says it intercepted Houthi missiles and drones attacking the kingdom. The Express Tribune, citing Bloomberg News, reports Saudi Arabia contained a fire near its Ras Tanura oil export terminal early today after an attack in the region. And Reuters reports Egypt has approved military support for Saudi Arabia.

In the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Reuters reports Ebola infections have passed ten thousand, according to the government. Reuters also reports protesters killed a longtime nurse who was treating Ebola patients.

In Sudan, a drone strike on a market in Nyala, in Darfur, killed twenty one people and wounded at least forty, a medical source and witnesses told A F P, as reported by Dawn. Reuters reports drone strikes also cut power across southeast Sudan, the power operator says.

A few updates on stories we've covered. In Nigeria, police told BBC Africa the death toll from the attack in Taraba State has risen to at least forty eight. In India, Reuters reports thousands of people have been evacuated and some trains cancelled, a day before Cyclone Arnab's expected landfall. In Nigeria, Reuters reports authorities are tracking fifty three contacts linked to an Ebola patient. And in the West Bank, the U.N. humanitarian office says partners are supporting more than three thousand Palestinians evacuated from their homes in Hebron by Israeli forces.

Now a few quick notes by region.

In Africa, BBC Africa reports Algeria will send troops to Niger. In Somalia, journalist Harun Maruf reports heavy fighting yesterday between federal forces and al Shabaab near Leego. Al Shabaab claims it killed more than three hundred soldiers in an ambush there. That is a militant claim, and no official figure has been reported.

In the Middle East, a drone strike on the Damascus to Homs highway in Syria yesterday killed three people, including Abu Abdul Rahman al Libi, a commander in the militant group Hurras al Din, a Syrian Defense Ministry source told A F P. In Iraq, the outlet Shafaq News reports a Baghdad court sentenced six French nationals to life in prison and a seventh to fifteen years for belonging to ISIS. And the Institute for the Study of War and the Critical Threats Project report officials from Mecca Pact countries met in Tehran yesterday.

In Europe, Reuters reports a Russian missile strike on Kyiv yesterday killed at least one person and injured ten, including students at a university dormitory, and Russian strikes on Kharkiv killed two. Radio Free Europe reports a humanitarian cease fire in Oleshky took effect early today. In Germany, journalist Florian Flade reports a German man was arrested at Frankfurt Airport on suspicion of trying to join the Islamic State in Somalia and Pakistan. And Australia's Smartraveller warns of protests in Spain.

In South Asia, Dawn reports Pakistan's Balochistan government banned gatherings in Pishin district with immediate effect.

On cyber, The Record reports the A I software project LiteLLM said a key accidentally exposed in May was behind last week's supply chain attack.
