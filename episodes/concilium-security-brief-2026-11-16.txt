Welcome to the Concilium Security Risk Brief for Monday, November sixteenth. Five minutes of security coverage from the last twenty four hours, to increase your situational awareness without decreasing your productivity. Here's what matters today.

Today: a new U.S. security alert for Bahrain, Houthi forces take control of the coast near the Bab el Mandeb Strait, drones strike ships in the Black Sea, deadly attacks on a foreign worker and a governor's aide in Nigeria, and an al Shabaab advance in central Somalia.

We start with Bahrain. The U.S. Embassy in Manama issued a security alert telling Americans in Bahrain to exercise heightened vigilance due to the increased threat of armed attacks by Iranian supported Houthis and Iranian efforts to target U.S. government officials and facilities in the region. The embassy says to be aware of potential alerts, flight cancellations, airspace closures and travel disruptions, and that it remains open for routine services.

Next, Yemen and the Red Sea. Reuters reports Yemeni government forces withdrew from an area near the Bab el Mandeb Strait, leaving the Houthis in control. The Institute for the Study of War says government aligned forces pulled back from most of their Red Sea coastal positions, likely to avoid encirclement and to preserve their ability to defend Aden. Reuters also reports the Houthis said they fired ballistic missiles toward King Khalid International Airport and a financial center in Riyadh, and the Saudi led coalition said it intercepted them. Separately, the International Committee of the Red Cross says authorities in Sanaa unilaterally released six hundred people detained in connection with the conflict, with support from its teams.

Turning to the Black Sea. Reuters reports three ships were struck by drones in the Black Sea near a Romanian shipping canal, citing the prime minister.

In Nigeria, the security analyst Zagazola Makama reports suspected terrorists killed a Chinese national and the police inspector escorting him in an ambush on a company vehicle in Ruga village, Alkaleri area, Bauchi state. Local reports say the attack happened on the night of Saturday, November fourteenth, with no official statement yet. And in Zamfara state, Nigerian broadcaster Galaxy T V reports suspected bandits attacked a mosque in Kaura Namoda during morning prayers yesterday, killing one man and abducting several worshippers. S twenty four Television identifies the man killed as a senior special assistant to the state governor.

And in Somalia. The Somali Guardian reports al Shabaab seized Coomaad, about a thirty minute drive from Mataban in the Hiiraan region, and is advancing toward a key central supply route. Kaab T V reports fierce fighting there between the Somali National Army and al Shabaab.

Now a few quick notes by region.

In Africa, Britain's Foreign Office updated its Madagascar travel advice to say Russia's state directed Africa Corps is present in the country and its forces may be visible in Antananarivo and elsewhere. The Foreign Office advises taking care if approached or stopped by Africa Corps personnel and remaining non confrontational.

In Europe, Radio Free Europe reports Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russian forces attacked at least nine regions overnight into yesterday, targeting energy, civilian and railway infrastructure and injuring at least sixteen people.

In the Americas, an update on Guatemala's thirty day state of emergency in Zacapa and Izabal: Canada updated its travel advice and says to expect an increased presence of security forces, follow the instructions of authorities and monitor official announcements. The U.S. State Department also posted an alert referring travelers to Guatemalan government sources for the full list of restrictions.
