Welcome to the Concilium Security Risk Brief for Tuesday, November twenty fourth. Five minutes of security coverage from the last twenty four hours, to increase your situational awareness without decreasing your productivity. Here's what matters today.

Today: a tighter British travel warning for parts of Ethiopia, a new Canadian warning for Riyadh's financial district, a car bomb in central Colombia, new U.S. sanctions on Iran's shadow banking networks, and a hospital hit during fighting in Mali.

We start in Ethiopia. The U.K. Foreign Office now advises against all but essential travel to East Borena and Arsi, in the Oromia region. It continues to advise against all travel, or all but essential travel, to other parts of the country.

Next, Saudi Arabia. Canada now says to avoid all travel to the King Abdullah Financial District in Riyadh, due to the risk of armed strikes, drone attacks and military debris.

Turning to Colombia. Noticias RCN reports a car bomb attack in central Villavicencio, near the Meta department governor's office, injured twenty three people. Colombian media report heavy damage to the governor's office, homes and businesses, and no deaths.

Next, sanctions. The U.S. Treasury says it sanctioned more than two dozen Iranian exchange houses, front companies and key personnel to disrupt Iran's shadow banking networks. In a separate analysis, the Treasury's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network identified nearly fifteen billion dollars in financial activity since January 2025 potentially linked to Iranian shadow banking.

And in Mali. On Saturday, November twenty first, a hospital in Niono, in the Ségou region, was hit during fighting, according to Doctors Without Borders. The group says two people were killed, including a health worker, and two other health workers, two caregivers and a child under five were wounded.

Now a few quick notes by region.

In Europe, Reuters reports schools across Germany and Denmark received bomb threats. In Ukraine, Reuters reports the regional governor says a Russian attack on Zaporizhzhia overnight killed two people and wounded forty, including four children. The U.N. humanitarian office says nearly thirty civilians were killed and two hundred forty injured across Ukraine between Friday and Monday morning.

On cyber, Reuters reports a European Commission academy platform was disabled after a cyber attack, according to a spokesperson. The Record reports two Norwegian energy companies, including Equinor, were affected by a Microsoft cloud security vulnerability that hackers have exploited to target credentials, according to Norway's cybersecurity authorities.

In the Middle East, Reuters reports Israeli strikes killed at least three people in Gaza, including a worker with the U.N. Palestinian refugee agency, UNRWA. Update on Iraq: the Institute for the Study of War says the government has not restored control over Jurf al Sakhr, where Kataib Hezbollah still has forces after a deal brokered by Iran's Revolutionary Guards. In Yemen, the Institute says the Saudi Air Force carried out airstrikes on Sunday, November twenty second, and Monday, likely to disrupt Houthi preparations for an offensive against Taiz city.

In South Asia, Reuters reports former Bangladeshi Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina plans to return home from exile in India on December sixteenth, according to her spokesperson.

In the Indian Ocean, the U.S. Geological Survey reports a preliminary magnitude six point four earthquake in the Mauritius and Réunion region early today.
