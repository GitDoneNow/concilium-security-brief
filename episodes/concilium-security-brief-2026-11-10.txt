Welcome to the Concilium Security Risk Brief for Tuesday, November tenth. Five minutes of security coverage from the last twenty four hours, to increase your situational awareness without decreasing your productivity. Here's what matters today.

Today: U.S. military aircraft moved out of a second British base over an Iranian threat, France formally blames Iran for sabotaging supermarket refrigerators, a police officer killed by a backpack bomb in northern Spain, polio vaccination workers abducted in northwest Pakistan, and a deadly drone attack on an army base in Colombia.

We start with the United Kingdom. Reuters reports, citing the Wall Street Journal, that the U.S. military moved refueling tankers out of R A F Mildenhall in eastern England on Monday after a threat from Iran, weeks after U.S. bombers were withdrawn from R A F Fairford. BBC Verify says flight tracking data shows at least ten U.S. Air Force tankers left Mildenhall on Monday. The Journal reports security at Mildenhall and nearby R A F Lakenheath was raised to the second highest force protection level. U.S. European Command said it could not discuss specific force protection measures.

Next, France. France's Foreign Ministry said Monday that an offensive cyber unit tied to Iran's Revolutionary Guards, called Shahid Kaveh, carried out the attacks that hit refrigeration controllers at more than seven hundred supermarkets and food sites across France on the night of October eleventh, causing fires and food losses estimated in the tens of millions of euros. Reuters reports Paris prosecutors have requested international arrest warrants for the unit's leader and two deputies, and France summoned Iran's ambassador and will propose new European Union sanctions. Separately, the State Department's travel service says protests continue across France, with demonstrations called for today, for Saturday, November fourteenth, and through the weekend, and that some past demonstrations turned violent.

Turning to Spain. Spanish public broadcaster R T V E reports a National Police officer was killed and a bomb disposal officer seriously injured this morning in Oviedo, when a backpack holding an explosive device blew up as officers moved it out of an apartment building. Police had responded to a shooting there earlier in the morning that wounded a seventeen year old girl. Police sources told the Spanish news agency E F E that the suspect's profile points away from a terrorist motive. The regional newspaper El Comercio reports police detained a man late this morning.

In Pakistan. The Hindu reports, citing police, that unidentified gunmen abducted three polio vaccination workers during an immunization campaign in Tank district in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, and took them to an undisclosed location.

And in Colombia. InSight Crime reports that explosives dropped from drones on the army base in Santa Rosa del Sur, in Bolívar department, on Saturday, November seventh, killed three soldiers and wounded sixteen. Colombian broadcaster Noticias Caracol puts the number of wounded at close to forty seven.

Now a few quick notes by region.

In Europe, Reuters reports Poland has arrested two Ukrainians over alleged espionage and sabotage on behalf of Russia. And The Record reports pro Russian hackers have published personal information about Italian Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani and other senior European officials on the dark web.

In the Middle East, Reuters reports the Saudi led coalition said it intercepted Houthi missiles and drones, while the Houthi run Saba news agency reported Saudi strikes on Sanaa. Reuters also reports Canada has reimposed sanctions on eleven Iranian entities for contributing to nuclear activities. In Gaza, Reuters reports four people were killed when a sand embankment collapsed onto tents, and the U.N. humanitarian office says heavy rain has flooded shelters across Gaza and triggered flash floods in Syria.

In Africa, an update on the attack on Cantine, in eastern Congo's North Kivu province, on the night of Thursday, November fifth. The Congolese news site Dépêche dot C D reports, citing local civil society, that the death toll has risen to forty one. Britain's Foreign Office has updated its Côte d'Ivoire travel advice with information on Ebola screening and health declarations on arrival. And BBC Africa reports nine Zimbabweans have been arrested in South Africa over alleged plans to join the war in Ukraine.

And in the Americas, Canada's travel advisory service says Pope Leo the Fourteenth is scheduled to visit Peru from November eleventh to sixteenth, and travelers should expect disruptions in several cities. The airport in Cusco will temporarily suspend operations on November fifteenth.
