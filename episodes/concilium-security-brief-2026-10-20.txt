We name the source for every item, so you can follow up on anything that affects your teams. Here's what you need to know today. There are five main items, then quick notes by region.

First, and most important for travel, Saudi Arabia. The State Department travel account says King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh is still shut after missile and drone attacks on airports in the Kingdom. Jeddah and Dammam are open, but with delays and cancellations. US government employees now need special approval from the Chief of Mission to use any airport in Saudi Arabia. The advice to US citizens is to expect possible attacks on airports, airspace closures and cancelled flights, to take extreme caution, and to follow Saudi Civil Defense alerts that come through as cell phone broadcasts. On the same story, the TRAC terrorism account reports the Houthis claiming new ballistic missile and drone attacks on several locations across Saudi Arabia. The Institute for the Study of War says the Houthis are changing tactics to get around Saudi air defenses, firing different weapons at the same time and going after places without air defense cover.

Second, Ebola. The World Health Organization says Nigeria has confirmed one imported case of Bundibugyo Ebola in the capital area around Abuja. The patient is a woman who had recently traveled from Uganda. She is in isolation, and forty four contacts are being monitored. Nigeria is the fifth country to detect this virus since the outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo was declared in May. The WHO is not advising travel restrictions. Separately, the UK Foreign Office travel advice account updated its Tanzania guidance on Ebola screening, and on a risk of protests on October twenty ninth.

Third, Kenya. The TRAC terrorism account reports an armed assault by al Shabaab on Christian civilians in Baure, in Lamu County on the coast. That is one report so far and it has not been confirmed, but it is the kind of incident teams working in Kenya will want to know about.

Fourth, Ethiopia. The Critical Threats Project says the Tigray People's Liberation Front has threatened to shoot down Ethiopian Airlines planes it believes are carrying military equipment or personnel. Federal forces are reportedly moving south from the Eritrean border toward Adwa and Adigrat, and rebel groups in Benishangul Gumuz and Oromia have carried out their first notable attacks since joining a new rebel coalition. Crisis Group warns that Ethiopian and Eritrean forces are sitting very close together, with a real risk of a direct clash.

Fifth, the Sahel. ACLED, citing Reuters, reports at least forty eight civilians killed in eastern Mali in attacks blamed on Islamic State linked militants. In Burkina Faso, the TRAC account reports al Qaeda linked JNIM fighters taking over a local militia base in Bam Province.

Now a few quick notes by region.

In the Middle East, the TRAC account reports that Israeli settlers attacked a visiting French mayor and the Archbishop of Cambrai in Taybeh, in the West Bank. That one matters for church groups visiting the area. The Long War Journal reports Iran saying Iraq has agreed to expel Kurdish Iranian opposition groups.

Elsewhere in Africa, there were al Shabaab attacks in Lower Shabelle and near Bosaso in Somalia, M23 clashes in North Kivu in Congo, and an Islamic State West Africa attack on the army in Borno, Nigeria, all from the TRAC account.

In Pakistan, the Pakistani Taliban claim twenty three attacks across Waziristan and nearby districts, and a roadside bomb hit a police vehicle in Quetta.

In Latin America, InSight Crime reports that a gang attack in Croix des Bouquets, Haiti, left four Salvadoran soldiers from the international force missing. Colombian forces defused a powerful roadside bomb on the Pan American Highway in Cauca, and cartel threat banners appeared in Zacatecas, Mexico.

In Europe, the State Department travel account warns of more protests across Italy after looting and clashes in Milan and Bologna. Australia's Smartraveller flags Serbia's elections on October twenty fifth, with protests that could turn violent.

On cyber, the cybersecurity agency CISA put out a joint advisory with the FBI on SafePay ransomware.

The US Geological Survey reported two strong quakes off Vanuatu, magnitude six point six and six point three. And nothing came through on Central Asia or Kyrgyzstan in this window.
