Welcome to the Concilium Security Risk Brief for Sunday, November fifteenth. Five minutes of security coverage from the last twenty four hours, to increase your situational awareness without decreasing your productivity. Here's what matters today.

Today: new Iranian drone attacks on Bahrain, more Houthi missiles toward Riyadh, death sentences in Jordan's church attack case, a state of emergency in Guatemala, and a deadly clash in northwest Nigeria.

We start with Bahrain. Reuters reports Bahrain intercepted Iranian aerial attacks this morning as Tehran said it attacked a U.S. base. Radio Free Europe reports sirens and blasts were heard across the kingdom, and Iran's Revolutionary Guards claim six drones targeted a U.S. radar system at the Riffa base. It is the second Iranian attack claim against U.S. assets in Bahrain in three days. Separately, A F P reports Iran's navy and Revolutionary Guards said fires broke out on three oil tankers that struck mines in the Strait of Hormuz. The Institute for the Study of War assesses those claims are likely meant to undermine ship operators' confidence in U.S. escort operations.

Next, Saudi Arabia and Yemen. Reuters reports the Saudi led coalition intercepted missiles fired toward Riyadh and drones over the Eastern Province. The Long War Journal reports the Houthis are sustaining missile and drone attacks across Saudi Arabia, and the Institute for the Study of War says the Houthis also fired ballistic missiles at Yemeni government forces' staging areas and weapons depots in Lahij and Aden.

Turning to Jordan. Reuters reports a Jordanian court sentenced three men to death over the deadly attack on a Greek Orthodox church in Karak on October twenty third.

In Guatemala, Reuters reports the government decreed a state of emergency in two departments after a lawmaker was killed in an attack. D W reports the thirty day measure covers Zacapa and Izabal in the east of the country.

And in Nigeria. A F P reports residents in Zamfara state say more than fifty people were killed on Thursday, November twelfth, when vigilantes from Zoma village in Bukkuyum district ambushed an armed gang returning from kidnappings in a neighboring district. A village chief put the dead at forty four bandits and nine vigilantes. Police said they had not yet received details.

Now a few quick notes by region.

In the Middle East, Reuters reports Hamas says a senior armed commander was killed in an Israeli strike. The group's armed wing named him as Samir Abu Younis, commander of its Rafah brigade.

In South Asia, Dawn reports Pakistan's military says security forces killed eight militants in Kolpur, Balochistan, and that on Saturday militants hit army patrol vehicles near Nushki with armed quadcopters, killing an army major and a non commissioned officer.

In the Americas, an update on Friday's nationwide U.S. flight ground stop: Reuters reports Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said a contractor's work switching telecommunication lines from copper to fiber led to the longest nationwide shutdown of flights since the September eleventh attacks. And Australia's Smartraveller issued information for Australians in Illinois after an explosive device was found Friday at an ethanol plant in Peoria. The F B I says it is leading the investigation and that no additional suspicious devices were found.
