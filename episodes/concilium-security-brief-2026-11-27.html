Today: two Italian workers killed in a drone attack in Kuwait, a deadly strike on a Saudi refinery in Jazan, a deadly night raid in Nigeria's Plateau State, arrests in Germany over a synagogue bomb plot, and new Australian sanctions over Iran-directed arson attacks.

We start in Kuwait. Reuters reports two Italian workers were killed in an attack on a site in Kuwait yesterday, with a source saying it was an Iranian strike. Al Jazeera reports Kuwait confirmed the deaths, and the Italian news agency ANSA says the two worked for the German defense company Rheinmetall. Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, rejected claims that Tehran was behind the attack and called the incident suspicious.

Turning to Saudi Arabia. Reuters reports an attack on Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery killed a worker and halted some operations yesterday. Al Jazeera, citing Saudi Arabia's energy ministry, says the worker was a Nepali national and that others were injured, and the Saudi-led coalition said the Houthis carried out the attack.

Next, Nigeria. TheCable and THISDAY report gunmen killed at least sixteen people in an overnight attack on the Tafi Gana community near Miango, in Bassa Local Government Area of Plateau State, on Wednesday night. Local media report the state government put the toll at seven killed and three injured, and that the governor directed security agencies to respond. The Tracking Terrorism and Counter Extremism group attributes the attack to Fulani herdsmen.

In Germany, federal prosecutors say police arrested two Iraqi nationals in Heidelberg and Cologne yesterday on suspicion of membership in a pro-Iranian terrorist group. Prosecutors say the group recruited people for bomb attacks, and that in late March unknown suspects, presumably carrying explosives, went to synagogues in Cologne and another European city, but no attack was carried out. Deutsche Welle reports more than two hundred fifty officers took part in searches at seventeen locations.

And in Australia, Reuters reports the government placed counterterrorism financing sanctions on two people it said orchestrated antisemitic arson attacks in Sydney and Melbourne on behalf of Iran.
