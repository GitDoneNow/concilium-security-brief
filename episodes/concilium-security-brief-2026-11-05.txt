Welcome to the Concilium Security Risk Brief for Thursday, November fifth. Five minutes of security coverage from the last twenty four hours, to increase your situational awareness without decreasing your productivity. Here's what matters today.

Today: a new wave of bomb threats against flights in Europe, the rising death toll from a Russian strike on an aid distribution point in Ukraine, a nationwide strike in France, a security operation against protesters in Pakistan administered Kashmir, and new U.S. sanctions on Iran's weapons and shipping networks.

We start with an update on Europe's aviation bomb threats. Reuters reports a bomb alert diverted an Airbus A320 flying from Dublin to Rome, according to the French prime minister, and a Finnair flight landed in Rome after a separate bomb alert this morning. The terrorism tracker TRAC says the new wave targeted at least twelve passenger flights across Europe.

Turning to Ukraine. Reuters reports the death toll from a Russian bombing of an aid distribution point in the Zaporizhzhia region on Monday, November second, has risen to thirty seven, with dozens still missing, officials said.

Next, France. The U.S. State Department says a nationwide multi sector strike is planned for today, affecting teachers, healthcare workers, government employees and public transport, with the four largest railway unions calling a joint national strike. It says recent demonstrations were violent and involved property destruction and looting, that local curfews in parts of France, including some Paris neighborhoods, could continue, and it advises avoiding demonstrations and large gatherings.

In Pakistan. Hum News reports security forces said they cleared a bus terminal in Rawalakot, in Azad Jammu and Kashmir, this morning, after an early morning operation against protesters who had occupied the area for about five months. Police in Poonch said they were removing illegal barricades, and police and protesters both reported injuries after an exchange of fire. The protest movement, the Joint Awami Action Committee, says three protesters were killed and twenty one injured.

And on sanctions. The U.S. Treasury says it targeted an India based network that provided false flags and certifications to sanctioned vessels carrying Iranian petroleum, and the State Department says the action covers thirteen individuals and companies, plus a Turkey based firm servicing sanctioned Iranian airlines. Separately, the State Department says it sanctioned two individuals and one entity involved in Iran's efforts to acquire shoulder fired air defense missiles.

Now a few quick notes by region.

In Africa, Critical Threats says the Rapid Support Forces and an allied militia captured the town of Sali, about eighty miles south of the Blue Nile state capital Ad Damazin in Sudan, on Tuesday, November third. In the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Radio Okapi reports hundreds of families fled a new attack attributed to the A D F armed group in the Bapere sector of Lubero territory on Tuesday, and Congolese media report twenty nine hostages held by the A D F were freed in joint Congolese and Ugandan army operations in Ituri.

In Europe, the Institute for the Study of War reports Russia's drone strikes on Ukrainian energy infrastructure overnight Tuesday into Wednesday heavily targeted central and western Ukraine and caused significant power outages across the country.

In the Middle East, Reuters reports Turkey's parliament is set to ratify a defense pact with Saudi Arabia and Pakistan as Houthi attacks mount.

In Asia, Reuters reports North Korea says it tested an air defense missile and staged a cruise missile drill.

In health, Reuters reports a British healthcare worker with Ebola is to be evacuated from Congo to the United Kingdom, according to a document it has seen. The U.K. Foreign Office updated its Ghana travel advice with information on Ebola screening.

And in cyber, The Record reports a suspected Russian cyberattack disrupted the website of the Kyiv Independent, one of Ukraine's leading English language news outlets. In an update on the wave of attacks on refrigeration systems across Europe, The Record reports temperature controls were altered at three sites of France's national blood service this week. It also reports a major Russian web hosting provider is struggling to restore services after a cyberattack destroyed data across its cloud infrastructure.
