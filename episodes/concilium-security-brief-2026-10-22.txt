Welcome to the Concilium Security Risk Brief for Thursday, October twenty second. Five minutes on the last twenty four hours of security developments, from government travel advisories and trusted analysts, curated by the Concilium Security Risk Management Team. Here's what matters today.

We start with a travel advisory change we hadn't yet covered. On Monday, October nineteenth, the State Department raised Kenya from Level 2, Exercise Increased Caution, to Level 3, Reconsider Travel. The advisory cites health, crime, kidnapping, terrorism, unrest and traffic safety, and it notes the CDC travel health notice for Ebola in Kenya. It says Do Not Travel to the Eastleigh and Kibera neighborhoods of Nairobi, to Garissa, Wajir, Mandera, Lamu and Tana River counties, northern Kilifi and the coast north of Malindi, to West Pokot and western Turkana, and to areas within fifty kilometers of the Ethiopian border in Marsabit and Turkana. It warns that al Shabaab is active in the border and coastal areas. This change could have repercussions for ministries and volunteer teams working in Kenya.

Next, Saudi Arabia and the Gulf. Reuters reports, citing three US officials, that the State Department was preparing an ordered departure of some personnel and family members from US embassies across the Gulf region, and later reported that the US has ordered diplomats to depart Saudi Arabia. The State Department travel account had not announced it as of this morning, so treat it as a single source report for now. Reuters also reports Saudi civil aviation saying one person was killed and eight injured in an attack on King Fahd airport in Dammam. And the TRAC terrorism account reports a Houthi attack on Saudi military cargo planes at Al Rayyan airport in Hadhramaut, Yemen.

On cyber, Reuters reports a critical vulnerability in PHP, the programming language behind a large share of the world's websites, led governments across Europe to take sites offline until a patch is installed, and Asian governments are stepping up their defenses. The Institute for the Study of War took its own website offline as a precaution after warnings from US cyber authorities. Separately, Microsoft says a China state aligned group it calls Storm zero nine forty used an AI penetration testing tool to automate attacks on hundreds of organizations worldwide, and was behind the recent ConnectWise supply chain compromise.

On sanctions, the Treasury Department sanctioned a Panama and Greece based law firm, along with shipping agents and vessels, for supporting illicit Iranian shipping.

A few updates on stories we've covered. Update on the Ebola outbreak: Australia's Smartraveller says Nigeria has introduced strict entry requirements after its confirmed case in Abuja. Update on Typhoon Champi: Reuters reports Taiwan issued a land warning and cancelled more than a hundred flights as the storm passes its northeastern coast. Update on northern Ethiopia: the UK Foreign Office has updated its travel advice with new information on the conflict, including the Tigray region.

Now a few quick notes by region.

In the Middle East, Reuters reports US congressional candidate Brad Lander was detained by Israeli forces in the West Bank, along with other Americans and Israelis who were harvesting olives with Palestinian farmers, according to rights groups. The UN humanitarian office, OCHA, says nearly two hundred sixty thousand people in Yemen have been displaced by fighting since August, half of them seeking refuge in Taiz. The Institute for the Study of War says Iran may have used more accurate weapons against commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz over the past two weeks.

In Africa, TRAC reports al Shabaab assaults on militia bases near Kismayo and on a Ugandan military base in Lower Shabelle, Somalia, and an Islamic State attack on Congolese army forces in Ituri province, Congo.

In South Asia, TRAC reports suspected Pakistani Taliban militants abducted sugar mill employees from a bus in Dera Ismail Khan district, an Islamic State Khorasan killing of a Pakistani intelligence officer in Bajaur, and attacks on Taliban police and checkpoints in Afghanistan's Ghazni and Parwan provinces.

In Latin America, TRAC reports an army soldier died after an ELN linked roadside bomb in Arauca, Colombia, and security forces captured an ELN drone operator in Cesar department.

In Europe, Reuters reports Germany's military is warning of a significant Russian sabotage threat. ACLED says protests in France have surged to around sixty five a day, more than three times the usual average, with nearly one in four involving riots.
