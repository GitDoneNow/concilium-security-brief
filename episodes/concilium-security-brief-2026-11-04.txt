Concilium Security Risk Brief

Welcome to the Concilium Security Risk Brief for Wednesday, November fourth. Five minutes of security coverage from the last twenty four hours, to increase your situational awareness without decreasing your productivity. Here's what matters today.

Today: a wave of bomb threats against flights across Europe, drone attacks on Iranian Kurdish opposition groups in northern Iraq, a state of emergency in parts of Belize, an update on Iran and the Strait of Hormuz, and a deadly ambush on security forces in northwest Nigeria.

We start in Europe. Reuters reports bomb threats against passenger flights and a cruise ship disrupted travel across Europe yesterday, and no devices were found. AFP puts the number of targeted flights at about ten, and says the threats forced the brief closure of a Warsaw airport and the scrambling of air force planes in Rome. Reuters says Warsaw Modlin airport reopened after its threat, and a second flight was checked at Warsaw Chopin.

Turning to Iraq. Reuters reports, citing sources, that drones attacked Iranian Kurdish opposition groups in Sulaymaniyah and east of Erbil, in the Kurdistan region, overnight.

Next, Belize. Canada updated its Belize travel advice to add a thirty day state of emergency declared on Saturday, October thirty first, in parts of Belize City, Belize District and Cayo District, following a sharp rise in violent crime. Canada says security forces there can restrict movement, conduct searches and detain people, and advises travelers in those areas to expect more police and military, carry identification and plan for transport delays. Canada's overall level is unchanged: exercise a high degree of caution, and avoid non essential travel to Southside Belize City.

An update on Iran. The Institute for the Study of War and Critical Threats assess that Iranian forces attempted to shoot down a U.S. aircraft near Qeshm Island, in the Strait of Hormuz, on Monday, November second.

And in Nigeria. Reuters, citing local security officials, reports at least eight people were killed when gunmen ambushed a convoy carrying security personnel near Gidan Giye in the Tsafe area of Zamfara State yesterday, including five police officers and three members of a state protection guard. The terrorism tracker TRAC attributes the attack to a bandit group and says several others were kidnapped.

Now a few quick notes by region.

In the Middle East, Reuters reports Syria says it foiled a Hezbollah plot to attack the oil refinery in Homs. The Long War Journal reports Lebanon's military prosecutor charged thirteen people over a Hezbollah explosives shipment.

In Europe, the Institute for the Study of War reports Russia carried out a large combined drone and missile strike on Ukrainian energy infrastructure overnight Sunday into Monday. Reuters reports the Russian held Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant says a Ukrainian drone killed two people, including a station employee, and that Latvia believes one of two drones that entered its airspace was Russian and the other Ukrainian. In Northern Ireland, the Belfast Telegraph reports police said a large haul of explosive devices found in Belfast's Shankill Road area over the weekend is linked to extreme far right terrorism.

In Africa, Critical Threats says two conflict trackers and an Ethiopian government aligned official alleged that Ethiopian and Eritrean forces exchanged fire on their shared border, though it could not confirm the claims. B B C Africa reports Uganda arrested former deputy military chief Peter Elwelu over alleged activities aimed at destabilizing the country. And the U.N. humanitarian office says El Niño floods are hitting Somalia, with some three point four million people possibly at risk of flooding by the end of the year.

In South Asia, Pakistan's A R Y News reports police in Lakki Marwat, in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, killed two militants, including a suicide bomber.

In health, the C D C's rule requiring everyone boarding a flight to the United States to attest they have not been in the Democratic Republic of the Congo in the previous twenty one days took effect at eleven fifty nine last night, Eastern time. Australia says travelers to Kenya may need to complete a health declaration and undergo screening on arrival because of the Ebola outbreak, and the U.K. updated its Madagascar advice with Ebola entry requirements and health screening.

And in cyber, Microsoft says it has identified a fake download campaign that targets people searching for cracked software, free games and plugins with trojanized installers. In an update on the Binance theft, Reuters reports the exchange says hackers used a bogus job interview and software hidden in an advertisement to compromise its engineers' computers.
