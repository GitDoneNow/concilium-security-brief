Concilium Security Risk Brief

Welcome to the Concilium Security Risk Brief for Friday, November twentieth. Five minutes of security coverage from the last twenty four hours, to increase your situational awareness without decreasing your productivity. Here's what matters today.

Today: Australia lowers its advice for Oman and Jordan, Iraqi forces move into a Kataib Hezbollah stronghold, a Houthi strike hits Riyadh's tallest tower, a deadly attack on Kyiv, and new U.S. sanctions on Southeast Asian scam networks.

First, travel advice. Smartraveller, Australia's travel advisory service, has lowered its overall level for Oman to exercise a high degree of caution, citing the unpredictable security situation in the region. It still advises reconsidering travel to areas within thirty kilometers of the Yemen border. Smartraveller also lowered Jordan to exercise a high degree of caution, citing the regional situation and the threat of terrorism, and still advises reconsidering travel to the Syrian and Iraqi border regions.

Turning to Iraq. Reuters reports Iraqi security forces began deploying in Jurf al Sakhr, a stronghold of the Kataib Hezbollah armed group south of Baghdad, under a compromise deal. Al Arabiya reports the spokesperson for the commander in chief of Iraq's armed forces said the redeployment is proceeding normally. Al Jazeera reports the government had given the group until today to withdraw.

Next, an update on the Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia. Reuters reports Riyadh's tallest skyscraper, the headquarters of the Saudi sovereign wealth fund, was struck in a Houthi attack. Separately, the State Department issued an alert for Kuwait telling Americans in the Middle East to exercise heightened vigilance, citing the threat of attacks by Iranian supported Houthis and Iranian efforts to target U.S. government officials and facilities.

In Ukraine, Radio Free Europe Radio Liberty reports Russia launched a combined drone and ballistic missile attack on Kyiv, killing at least three people and injuring fifteen, according to local officials.

And on sanctions, the Treasury Department's Office of Foreign Assets Control sanctioned cyber scam operators and armed group facilitators in Southeast Asia. The State Department says the scam centers are run mostly by Chinese organized crime and fuel human trafficking. Treasury also sanctioned two drug trafficking organizations, the Australia based Comanchero Outlaw Motorcycle Gang and Italy's 'Ndrangheta.

Now a few quick notes by region.

In the Middle East, an update on the Strait of Hormuz. Al Arabiya and Iran International report Iran's Revolutionary Guard says two supertankers hit naval mines and exploded in the strait. And the Institute for the Study of War assesses the Houthis may be conducting an offensive in Yemen's Abyan province to tie down government aligned forces that could reinforce Aden.

In Africa, the U.S. Embassy in Ethiopia says violence continues in remote parts of Tigray near the border with Eritrea and the security situation may change at any time. In Sudan, the U.N. humanitarian office says air strikes reportedly hit a village in the Kordofan region, killing fifteen civilians, including four children.

In Asia, Reuters reports Cyclone Akvan made landfall on India's eastern coast with heavy rain, the third such storm to hit the country in less than a month. And BBC Africa reports the International Court of Justice ruled that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya, in a case brought by The Gambia.

In Europe, Travel Canada says all border crossings between Russia and Estonia will close starting December first. In cyber news, The Record reports Ukrainian authorities arrested four suspected members of an international ransomware network accused of extorting more than forty million dollars from companies worldwide.

In the Americas, on Wednesday, November eighteenth, Salvadoran President Nayib Bukele announced that Salvadoran troops destroyed the house of a gang leader in Haiti, RFI and DW report.
