Security Risk Brief: Wednesday, October 21, 2026
Concilium Security Risk Brief

Welcome to the Concilium Security Risk Brief for Wednesday, October twenty first. Five minutes on the last twenty four hours of security developments, from government travel advisories and trusted analysts, curated by the Concilium Security Risk Management Team. Here's what matters today.

Our top story is Ebola in Africa. The U.S. State Department's travel account reports that Nigeria confirmed a case of Bundibugyo virus disease, a form of Ebola, on October twentieth. The patient traveled by air from Uganda and is in the Federal Capital Territory, and health officials are tracing contacts. The World Health Organization says Nigeria is now the fifth country to detect the virus since the outbreak was declared in the Democratic Republic of the Congo in May. BBC Africa adds that Kenya has confirmed nine cases linked to one man who traveled from Congo through Uganda undiagnosed, and a doctors' union there warns of understaffed border crossings. The UK Foreign Office also updated its Tanzania advice on Ebola screening. If you have teams in East or West Africa, check screening rules at your entry points and review your medical evacuation plans.

Second, Sudan. BBC Africa reports that Sudan's army is accused of drone strikes that reportedly killed more than two hundred people, including dozens travelling to a funeral. The linked BBC report cites sources putting the toll above three hundred, with a fuel market also hit. These figures have not been independently confirmed.

Third, the Sahel. ACLED highlighted a Reuters report that Islamic State linked militants killed at least forty eight civilians in eastern Mali. Separately, TRAC reports that JNIM militants overran a pro government militia base at Malou in Burkina Faso's Bam Province.

Fourth, northern Ethiopia. The Critical Threats Project reports Ethiopian federal forces are redeploying from the Eritrean border toward Tigray's main towns, including Adwa and Adigrat, while Tigrayan rebel forces keep up attacks. The UN humanitarian office, OCHA, says renewed fighting is worsening an already dire situation, and Crisis Group warns of a risk of direct confrontation between Ethiopia and Eritrea.

Fifth, Italy. The State Department warns that protests may occur across Italy over the next few days, after recent demonstrations in Milan and Bologna brought looting and violent clashes, especially in city squares and near main railway stations. Australia's Smartraveller issued a similar caution, and the UK Foreign Office added a national strike planned for October thirtieth.

Now a few quick notes by region.

In the Middle East, the State Department reminded U.S. citizens in Saudi Arabia to follow civil defense alerts sent by cellular broadcast, and noted that U.S. government staff there need special authorization to use any airport. The Institute for the Study of War says Houthi and Yemeni government aligned forces are still contesting high ground over western Taiz, and that the Houthis likely fired ballistic missiles at Marib City on October nineteenth. Long War Journal reports Iran says it has an agreement with Iraq to expel Kurdish Iranian opposition groups.

In South Asia, TRAC reports the Pakistani Taliban claimed more than twenty attacks across North and South Waziristan, Bannu, Tank and Pishin. Radio Free Europe reports rising militant attacks along the Iran Pakistan border.

In Africa, TRAC reports al Shabaab assaults on militia bases in Somalia's Lower Shabelle region, an Islamic State West Africa attack on Nigerian troops in Borno State, and M23 clashes with Congolese forces in North Kivu. BBC Africa reports residents near Pretoria, South Africa, were told not to drink or cook with tap water after more than four hundred people fell ill. The UK Foreign Office flagged a risk of protests in Tanzania on October twenty ninth.

In Latin America, InSight Crime reports an attack in Croix des Bouquets, Haiti, left four Salvadoran soldiers with the international force missing. In Mexico, InSight Crime reports five alleged Chapitos members were arrested, and TRAC reports four public officials in Taxco, Guerrero, were arrested over cartel links. In Colombia, TRAC reports security forces defused a powerful roadside bomb on the Pan American Highway in Cauca.

In Europe and Asia, Global Affairs Canada warns of possible demonstrations around Serbia's October twenty fifth elections and is tracking Typhoon Champi near Okinawa, Japan.

On cyber, the U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, with the FBI and Secret Service, issued an advisory on SafePay ransomware targeting businesses and critical infrastructure worldwide. Nonprofits should review it too.
