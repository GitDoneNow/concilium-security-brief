Concilium Security Risk Brief

Welcome to the Concilium Security Risk Brief for Wednesday, October twenty first. Five minutes of security coverage from the last twenty four hours, to increase your situational awareness without decreasing your productivity. Here's what matters today.

We start in Nigeria. The World Health Organization says Nigeria has confirmed one imported case of Bundibugyo virus disease, a form of Ebola, in the Federal Capital Territory around Abuja. Nigeria is the fifth country to detect the virus since the outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo was declared in May. The patient is a woman who recently travelled from Uganda, and forty four contacts are being monitored. The WHO advises against travel or trade restrictions, and the State Department travel account says health officials are conducting contact tracing.

In Mali, Reuters, in a report shared by ACLED, says at least forty eight civilians were killed in eastern Mali in attacks attributed to Islamic State linked militants. ACLED data show clashes between the Islamic State Sahel Province and al Qaeda affiliates have left more than two thousand dead since twenty nineteen.

In Sudan, BBC Africa reports the army has been accused of launching several drone strikes that reportedly killed more than two hundred people, including dozens travelling to a funeral.

In Ethiopia, the Critical Threats Project says the Tigray People's Liberation Front warned the federal government against using airports for military purposes, and threatened to shoot down Ethiopian Airlines planes carrying suspected military equipment and personnel. Federal forces are reportedly redeploying from the Eritrean border toward the Tigrayan cities of Adwa and Adigrat. The UN humanitarian office says renewed fighting is making an already dire humanitarian situation worse.

In Italy, the State Department travel account says protests may occur across the country over the next few days. Recent demonstrations in Milan and Bologna led to looting and violent clashes, and more demonstrators may gather in city center squares and near main railway stations. Australia says even peaceful demonstrations can escalate quickly, and the UK Foreign Office has added information about a national strike planned for October thirtieth.

A few updates on stories we've covered. On Ebola in Kenya, BBC Africa reports Kenya has now confirmed nine cases linked to one man who travelled from Congo through Uganda, and a doctors' union warns of understaffed border crossings.

On Saudi Arabia, the State Department travel account says US government employees there need special authorization to use any airport in the country, and reminds US citizens to follow Saudi Civil Defense alerts sent by cellular broadcast. Dawn reports the Saudi led coalition said it intercepted a Houthi ballistic missile targeting Riyadh, and that the UK will deploy Typhoon fighter jets to defend Saudi cities.

Update on Yemen: the Critical Threats Project and the Institute for the Study of War say Houthi and government-aligned forces are likely still contesting Jabal Han, a mountain overlooking western Taiz City. The Houthis likely fired several ballistic missiles at Marib City on October nineteenth.

Now a few quick notes by region.

In the Middle East and North Africa, Reuters reports Iraq has closed the Sheeb border crossing with Iran to passengers, vehicles and commercial trucks until further notice. Long War Journal reports Iran says it has an agreement with Iraq to expel Kurdish Iranian opposition groups. And Reuters says Turkey is planning a mandate to send military personnel to Saudi Arabia.

In Africa, BBC Africa reports former Rwandan army sergeant major Robert Kabera, a critic of President Paul Kagame, was found beheaded in neighbouring Uganda. Residents near Pretoria, South Africa, have been told not to drink or cook with tap water after more than four hundred people, mostly young children, fell ill. And the UK Foreign Office updated its Tanzania advice on Ebola screening and the risk of protests on October twenty ninth.

In the Americas, InSight Crime reports an attack in Croix-des-Bouquets, Haiti, a stronghold of the four hundred Mawozo gang, left four Salvadoran soldiers missing.

In Asia Pacific, Canada is tracking Typhoon Champi, expected to sweep across the Ryukyu Islands and Okinawa in Japan. Reuters reports North Korea's army chief inspected the border with South Korea and said troops must act if provoked. And Australian lawmaker Helen Haines had both legs amputated after being rammed by a car outside her electoral office. The accused has appeared in court.

In Europe, the Institute for the Study of War cites Ukraine's prime minister saying a Russian strike on a park in Nova Vodolaha, in Kharkiv province, killed at least six people, including two children, on October nineteenth. Russia launched one hundred sixty five drones at Ukraine overnight, and Reuters reports Russian drones killed two people near Kharkiv. In Russia, the governor of the Bryansk region says one person was killed in an attack.

On cyber, CISA, the FBI and the Secret Service issued an advisory on SafePay ransomware targeting businesses and critical infrastructure worldwide. Reuters reports a cyber agency warned websites may go dark as PHP prepares a critical fix, and State Bank of India reported a cyberattack involving nearly three thousand nine hundred customer records.
