Welcome to the Concilium Security Risk Brief for Friday, October twenty third. Five minutes of security coverage from the last twenty four hours, to increase your situational awareness without decreasing your productivity. Here's what matters today.

We start with an update on Saudi Arabia. Reuters and AFP report, citing sources, that Saudi Aramco told its remaining staff to evacuate its headquarters in Dhahran, in the Eastern Province, amid continuing Houthi attacks. There's no official confirmation yet. Canada now says to avoid all travel to King Fahd International Airport in Dammam because ongoing military activity is disrupting airport operations. And the TRAC terrorism account reports the Houthis are claiming missile and drone attacks on Abha International Airport, King Fahd airport in Dammam, the Aramco refinery in Yanbu, and the Saudi Ministry of Defense headquarters.

In Sudan, the UN humanitarian office, OCHA, says air and drone strikes are killing civilians, including humanitarian responders, while funding shortages threaten the aid response. The International Committee of the Red Cross reports a worrying increase in civilian harm from aerial attacks, with hundreds of people reportedly killed. And BBC Africa reports the UN human rights chief called a BBC investigation, which found evidence that the Rapid Support Forces systematically destroyed hospitals, deeply disturbing, saying the actions may amount to war crimes.

In China, the US Geological Survey reported a magnitude seven point zero earthquake in Tibet. Reuters reports sixteen people killed and sixty five injured.

In Kenya, TRAC reports al Shabaab militants carried out an armed assault on a Kenyan military base in Mandera County. TRAC's weekly watch also says al Shabaab has explicitly named Christians as targets in Kenya. This could have repercussions for ministries working in the area.

In Europe, the State Department warns that violence and rioting tied to ongoing protests in France have occurred and may continue. The UK Foreign Office updated its advice for France, Reunion and Guadeloupe with information about demonstrations, and Canada updated its advice for Italy after demonstrations disrupted services and led to vandalism and clashes with police.

A few updates on stories we've covered. Update on the Ebola outbreak: BBC Africa reports that both the Nigerian and Kenyan first cases passed through Uganda's Entebbe airport on October third. Africa CDC is investigating but says no link has been established. Reuters reports the Swiss government says the Nigeria patient did not travel to Congo. Update on Typhoon Champi: Reuters reports Taiwan shut down with hundreds of flights cancelled, and the storm is now approaching Japan. Update on Yemen: the Institute for the Study of War says the Houthis almost certainly captured a major road segment linking their front lines outside Taiz City to central Taiz Governorate. Update on Tigray, Ethiopia: ACLED says control continues to shift, with forces known as the TPF regaining the town of Abi Adi.

In the Middle East and North Africa, TRAC reports a security director in Hadhramaut, Yemen, survived a roadside bomb, and an appeals judge was assassinated. The State Department spokesperson says the US is disturbed by developments at Libya's Central Bank, warning of risks to the country's financial and economic stability. Reuters reports Israel released almost all suspects in two settler attacks on Palestinians to house arrest, and an Israeli rights group has documented almost one hundred seventy incidents against the olive harvest in recent weeks.

In Africa, TRAC reports an al Shabaab bomb at a militia checkpoint in Mogadishu, and a JNIM takeover of a militia post in southwestern Burkina Faso. BBC Africa reports police in Durban, South Africa, say a foreign national was beaten to death by a crowd of about fifty people.

In South and Central Asia, TRAC reports an Afghanistan Freedom Front bomb attack on a Taliban intelligence unit in Takhar province.

On cyber, The Record reports Interpol arrested more than fourteen hundred suspected cybercriminals across Africa this summer, in a crackdown on online fraud, ransomware and business email compromise.
