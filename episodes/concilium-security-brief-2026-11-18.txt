Today: Iran launches drones at Kuwait and Bahrain, a national strike called in Colombia for tomorrow, new Ebola figures and travel measures, a polio detection in Syria, and Iranian security forces deployed to major cities.

We start in the Gulf. Kuwait's armed forces said overnight that their air defenses were engaging drone threats, and urged the public to stay calm and follow official safety instructions, according to the Kuwait News Agency. Al Jazeera, citing Iranian state media, reports Iran's army said it targeted U.S. drone and missile installations at Ali Al Salem Air Base in Kuwait. In Bahrain, the Bahrain Defence Force said its air defenses intercepted several Iranian aerial attacks early today, Reuters and the Kuwait News Agency report. Al Jazeera reports the U.S. struck an Iranian missile site on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, and that six oil tankers have reportedly been damaged by sea mines in the strait. Saudi Arabia also condemned an attack on a U.A.E. oil tanker, Al Arabiya reports. Kuwait's Foreign Ministry condemned the repeated Iranian attacks on its territory.

Next, Colombia. The State Department says labor unions, social organizations and opposition groups have called a national strike and demonstrations for this Thursday, November nineteenth, in Bogotá, Medellín, Cali, Barranquilla and Bucaramanga, with more gatherings and marches possible in other cities.

An update on the Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. The U.N. humanitarian office says more than eleven thousand four hundred cases have been reported, and nearly half of those infected have died. Reuters reports the U.N. says insecurity is complicating efforts to contain the outbreak. Separately, Australia's Smartraveller says El Salvador now requires a twenty one day quarantine for health monitoring for travelers who visited or transited parts of Central or East Africa in the twenty one days before arrival.

In Syria, the World Health Organization says circulating variant poliovirus type one has been detected in Deir ez Zor governorate, and the health ministry, backed by the W H O and UNICEF, is scaling up vaccination and surveillance.

And in Iran, the Institute for the Study of War and Critical Threats assess that the regime has likely deployed security forces to some major Iranian cities in recent days, as part of preparations for potential anti regime protests.

Now a few quick notes by region.

In the Middle East, Reuters reports the Houthis say they launched drones toward Saudi Arabia's East West pipeline. The Kuwait News Agency reports the Saudi led coalition said it intercepted a Houthi cruise missile fired toward Riyadh. And Reuters reports Houthi run Al Masirah TV showed a factory in Hodeidah on fire after reported Saudi strikes.

In Europe, an update on Ukraine's strikes on the bridge to Crimea. The Institute for the Study of War says Ukrainian forces struck the Kerch Strait Bridge for a second straight night, completely collapsing its road sections and damaging the railway section. Reuters reports hoax bomb threats have hit more than eight thousand institutions across Europe over the past month, rattling schools and airports. And the U.S. Geological Survey reports a preliminary magnitude five point three earthquake in Romania.

In Africa, an update on the Ethiopia and Eritrea border. ACLED says Eritrean forces regained control of Badme over the weekend and advanced about twenty kilometers toward Tigray. Al Jazeera, drawing on ACLED data, reports renewed clashes between Somali police and Oromo militias in Moyale, Ethiopia, have displaced thousands.

In the Americas, the State Department says farmers are blocking highways at five locations in Jalisco outside Guadalajara, Mexico, to protest a lack of government support for agriculture.
