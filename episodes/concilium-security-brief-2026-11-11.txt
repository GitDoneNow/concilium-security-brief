Welcome to the Concilium Security Risk Brief for Wednesday, November eleventh. Five minutes of security coverage from the last twenty four hours, to increase your situational awareness without decreasing your productivity. Here's what matters today.

Today: hundreds of schools in Italy evacuated after a bomb alert, a U.S. soldier arrested in Britain on suspicion of spying for Russia, drone shelter alerts across the Baltic states, two storms hitting Portugal, and border clashes between Ethiopia and Eritrea.

We start with Italy. Reuters reports that at least three hundred schools in Italy were evacuated this morning after receiving the same bomb alert message, according to the education minister.

Next, the United Kingdom. The Record reports British authorities said a U.S. Army member was arrested on suspicion of spying for Russia, and BNO News reports the FBI confirmed the arrest. Separately, Reuters reports the U.S. Justice Department says an Iran backed militant helped plot an attack on R A F Fairford in England while he was held in a U.S. jail.

Turning to the Baltic states. Reuters reports Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia issued a flurry of alerts urging people to seek shelter from unidentified drones entering from Russia or Belarus, then withdrew them, officials and media said. Reuters also reports Latvia and Lithuania have warned of new drone threats, as Finland adopts new curbs.

In Portugal. The State Department's travel service says Storm Bruna is bringing rain, strong winds and rough seas to mainland Portugal through Friday, and a second storm, Charles, is expected to reach the mainland today with heavy rain, winds of up to one hundred forty kilometers per hour, and a risk of river overflows and flash flooding. Britain's Foreign Office has also updated its Portugal travel advice with information on severe weather.

And in Ethiopia and Eritrea. The Critical Threats Project says several conflict trackers reported clashes between Ethiopian and Eritrean forces near the Eritrean border town of Badme on Monday, November ninth, though it could not confirm which side of the border the fighting took place on. It assesses that the military buildup and clashes are likely becoming a sustained, low intensity border war.

Now a few quick notes by region.

In Europe, Reuters reports a drone attack left part of the Ukrainian city of Kryvyi Rih without power.

In the Middle East, Reuters reports an Israeli airstrike on a metal foundry in Gaza City killed three people, and four more people, including a thirteen year old, were killed in other parts of Gaza City and in Khan Younis, medics said.

In South Asia, Pakistan's Tribal News Network reports militants opened fire from the surrounding mountains on police in the Ghambat area of Kohat district, in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, this morning, as officers headed out for an operation against illegal mines.

In Africa, BBC Africa reports a military court in the Democratic Republic of the Congo has sentenced sixty six soldiers to death for cowardice. And the Long War Journal reports Ugandan troops have begun withdrawing from Somalia, and U.S. forces have left a key base there.

In the Americas, the U.N. humanitarian office says armed violence in Arcahaie and Cabaret, in Haiti, has displaced more than five thousand nine hundred people since late October. The Miami Herald reports Haitian police and Salvadoran troops destroyed the house of the leader of the 400 Mawozo gang in Croix des Bouquets on Sunday, November eighth.

And in cyber, The Record reports Russian state linked hackers compromised government email servers in Romania and Kazakhstan to secretly intercept messages with NATO and European Union officials, according to new research. Microsoft says it is tracking a Brazil focused threat cluster it calls Storm thirty three fifty, mainly affecting financial services, payments, banking and digital assets, as well as healthcare, transportation and logistics.
