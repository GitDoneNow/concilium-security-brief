Concilium Security Risk Brief

Welcome to the Concilium Security Risk Brief for Saturday, November twenty first. Five minutes of security coverage from the last twenty four hours, to increase your situational awareness without decreasing your productivity. Here's what matters today.

Today: a strong earthquake in central Peru, two tanker attacks near the Strait of Hormuz, Russian missiles and a drone in Polish airspace, new U.S. sanctions on Iran's oil trade, and planned protests across France this weekend.

We start in Peru. The U.S. Geological Survey reported a magnitude six point seven earthquake in the Junín region of central Peru on Friday evening, at a depth of more than one hundred kilometers. Peru's civil defense institute, INDECI, reports damage to homes, a hospital in Pichanaqui, churches and roads across Junín, Pasco, Huánuco and Lima, along with power outages, and rockfalls restricting traffic on routes including the Lima to Huancayo highway. In Lima, rocks fell onto the Costa Verde coastal road. State broadcaster TV Peru reports the head of INDECI said no injuries had been reported.

Turning to the Strait of Hormuz. Reuters reports two tanker attack incidents near Khasab, Oman, today, according to the U.K. Maritime Trade Operations center. Separately, the Institute for the Study of War assesses Iran will likely attempt to run the U.S. naval blockade in the coming days.

Next, Poland. Reuters reports three Russian objects, believed to be two missiles and a drone, violated Polish airspace overnight into Friday, and Poland's defense minister called it a provocation by Moscow. The Institute for the Study of War assesses the Kremlin conducted the violations intentionally, to coincide with an ongoing Coalition of the Willing military exercise. In Ukraine, an update on Friday's overnight attack: Radio Free Europe Radio Liberty reports at least nine people were killed and twenty eight injured across the country.

On sanctions. The Treasury Department says it is targeting key oil trustees the Iranian regime relies on to sell oil abroad, as part of what it calls Operation Economic Outcast. The State Department spokesperson said four Iranian regime insiders were sanctioned. Reuters also reports Treasury is pressing Singapore and Malaysia on Iran sanctions compliance.

And in France, the State Department says protests and rallies continue across the country, with multiple groups planning demonstrations for today and tomorrow. It notes protests can occur outside planned routes, and some past demonstrations were violent and involved property destruction and looting.

Now a few quick notes by region.

In the Middle East, Smartraveller updated its Saudi Arabia advice, still at do not travel. It says the Houthis have targeted or threatened energy facilities, offices, airports and Red Sea ports, including in Riyadh and Dammam, and that Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District has also been targeted.

In Africa, an update on Ethiopia. Critical Threats says the Ethiopian military reportedly resumed fighting with Eritrean and Tigray People's Liberation Front forces around the Eritrean border town of Badme. In Sudan, ACLED and Critical Threats say the Sudanese Armed Forces have made significant westward advances in central Sudan, and ACLED says the army's advances toward Al Nuhud in West Kordofan are putting pressure on R S F positions.

In Asia, Thai P B S World reports suspected insurgents carried out coordinated early morning attacks in Narathiwat province in Thailand's deep south, setting off explosions, burning tires and targeting surveillance cameras. And the U.S. Geological Survey reported a magnitude six point eight earthquake in the Banda Sea, northeast of Timor Leste.

In Central Asia, Travel Canada says the Bedel border crossing between Kyrgyzstan and China is closed, and that crossings may close without notice.

In Europe, the State Department says a public transport strike with a union protest is scheduled in Belgium for Monday, November twenty third.

In the Americas, the U.K. Foreign Office says Ecuador has extended its state of emergency for another thirty days in eight provinces, including Guayas, Pichincha and Esmeraldas, with greater police and military presence on the streets.

On aviation security, Times Now reports the F B I, the Department of Homeland Security and the T S A warned U.S. airlines, cargo carriers and airports about al Qaeda messages calling for attacks on aircraft, including hijackings like September's attempted hijacking of a Tel Aviv bound Flydubai flight.

And on cyber threats, The Record reports hackers exploited a software supplier's vulnerability to breach more than forty Irish credit unions. Reuters, citing NBC News, reports hackers breached the U.S. Department of Agriculture.
