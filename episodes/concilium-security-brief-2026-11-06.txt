Today: Canada's expanded avoid non essential travel advice for parts of coastal Ecuador, another bomb threat diverting a Prague to Rome flight, the rising death toll from the Zaporizhzhia aid point strike, a Saudi civil defence alert at the Yanbu oil hub, and a U.S. government warning on cyberattacks against industrial refrigeration systems.

We start with Ecuador. Canada says it has changed the risk level for certain cantons in the provinces of Guayas, Manabi and Santa Elena to avoid non essential travel, due to the risks of violent crime. The updated page lists areas including Duran, Milagro, Naranjal, Yaguachi, parts of Guayaquil, Manta, Portoviejo, La Libertad and Santa Elena. Canada's overall advice for Ecuador remains exercise a high degree of caution, and the Galapagos Islands remain take normal security precautions.

Next, an update on Europe's aviation bomb threats. Reuters reports a bomb scare forced the diversion of a Prague to Rome flight to Venice this morning, continuing the wave of hoax threats against European flights covered yesterday.

Turning to Ukraine. Reuters reports the death toll from the Russian bombing of an aid distribution point in the Zaporizhzhia region on Monday, November second, has risen to forty three. Rescuers found one survivor three days after the strike, and police said up to thirty three people may still be buried under the rubble in the village of Komyshuvakha.

In Saudi Arabia. Reuters reports Saudi civil defence warned of potential danger in Yanbu, the kingdom's Red Sea oil export hub, as Houthi attacks continue. The Saudi led coalition said it destroyed a missile launched toward the Medina region, and civil defence also issued emergency alerts for Sharurah near the Yemen border.

And on cyber. The U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency and federal partners say malicious cyber actors are targeting industrial refrigeration and cold chain monitoring systems, and they published a joint fact sheet outlining steps operators should take. Reuters and The Record report the warning follows recent hacks that disrupted temperature controls at stores across Europe.

In Africa, the U.K. Foreign Office updated its Uganda travel advice with information on precautionary measures in response to the Ebola outbreak in the region.

In Europe, Reuters reports Russian strikes caused new power outages in seven Ukrainian regions, with the Kyiv area worst hit.

In the Americas, the State Department says it is offering rewards of up to fifty million dollars for information leading to the arrests or convictions of Tren de Aragua leaders it names as Yohan Petrica, Giovanni and Juancho.

In health, Reuters reports a British doctor with Ebola has returned from Congo to London for treatment.

And in cyber, Microsoft Threat Intelligence says it has observed threat actors using agentic A I tools to search public GitHub repositories for exposed secrets. The Record reports Russian state linked hackers targeted users in Ukraine, the United States and Europe in a campaign designed to steal sensitive information from iPhones and Android devices.
