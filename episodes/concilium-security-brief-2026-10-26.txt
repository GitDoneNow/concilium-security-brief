Welcome to the Concilium Security Risk Brief for Monday, October twenty sixth. Five minutes of security coverage from the last twenty four hours, to increase your situational awareness without decreasing your productivity. Here's what matters today.

We start with a travel health order from the United States. The C D C says that beginning Tuesday, November third, at eleven fifty nine p.m. Eastern Standard Time, all passengers boarding an international flight to the United States, including those connecting through the U.S., must sign a statement that they have not been in the Democratic Republic of the Congo in the twenty one days before boarding. That includes passing through an airport in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, even without leaving the airport. Airlines must confirm each attestation before boarding, deny boarding to passengers without one unless they have a U.S. government exemption, and keep the attestations for at least forty two days. The C D C says willfully false statements may lead to criminal fines and imprisonment.

In Nigeria, on Friday, October twenty third, gunmen on motorcycles attacked the village of Binnari, in the Karim Lamido area of Taraba State. BBC Africa and The Guardian Nigeria report police say at least ten people were killed, and witnesses say the toll could be much higher. Channels Television reports at least thirty seven confirmed dead, and Amnesty International says at least forty were killed. Shops were looted and homes set on fire.

In Saudi Arabia, the Institute for the Study of War and the Critical Threats Project report the Houthis have fired at least twelve missiles and two drones at Saudi Arabia since Saturday. Australia's Smartraveller says it continues to advise Do Not Travel to Saudi Arabia, and not to travel to King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, King Fahd International Airport in Dammam, or Abha International Airport, because recent security incidents are disrupting airport operations.

In India, Reuters reports two states on the eastern coast are preparing to evacuate thousands of people ahead of Cyclone Arnab, which is expected to make landfall on Wednesday. One of the states says it will close schools in some districts for two days.

In Ukraine, Reuters reports President Zelenskiy says US warnings of imminent Russian ballistic missile strikes have been fully restored after recent issues. Officials say Russian strikes injured six people in the Zaporizhzhia and Chernihiv regions. Inside Russia, the governor of the Voronezh region says a drone attack killed two men.

A few updates on stories we've covered. Update on the church attack in Jordan: Reuters reports, citing state television, that Jordanian security forces have arrested three suspects in the October twenty third attack in the Karak area, which killed nine people. Update on Ebola, beyond the U.S. order we covered at the top: Reuters reports Nigeria's Ebola patient has been medically evacuated to Switzerland, and the Africa C D C says scientists will investigate where the Nigeria case came from. Uganda says citizens returning with a history of travel to the Democratic Republic of the Congo will be quarantined for twenty one days, according to Reuters and BBC Africa.

Now a few quick notes by region.

In Africa, NewsPoint Nigeria and Dawn Herald report bandits killed seven members of a local vigilante group and abducted dozens of residents in an overnight raid on Kaura Namoda, in Zamfara State, starting Friday, October twenty third.

In South Asia, Pakistan's military media wing says security forces killed six militants in an operation in Zarkani, in Dera Ismail Khan district, on Saturday, October twenty fourth, and that two soldiers were killed.

In the Pacific, Smartraveller says Vanuatu's Ministry of Health has declared an influenza A outbreak in Port Vila and on Efate Island.

In Europe, Radio Free Europe reports Serbia's Aleksandar Vucic conceded defeat to the student led protest movement in yesterday's parliamentary elections. Final results have not yet been announced.

On sanctions, a Reuters exclusive reports how Myanmar disguised purchases of sanctioned Iranian jet fuel, including a December twenty twenty five delivery by the US sanctioned tanker Reef.
