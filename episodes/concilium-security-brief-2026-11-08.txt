Welcome to the Concilium Security Risk Brief for Sunday, November eighth. Five minutes of security coverage from the last twenty four hours, to increase your situational awareness without decreasing your productivity. Here's what matters today.

Today: a deadly mass shooting in Krakow's main square, falling debris damaging an Aramco building in Riyadh, U.S. strikes and a disabled ship in the Iran blockade, a heavy Russian missile strike on Kyiv, and a deadly attack on a lawmaker in Guatemala.

We start with Poland. Reuters reports a gunman shooting from a car killed six people in Krakow's historic main square on Saturday afternoon. Authorities said seventeen people were wounded, including two police officers and the gunman, and the suspect's parents and sister were later found dead at their home. Authorities said the attack was likely inspired by far right ideology. Poland began two days of national mourning today, police have bolstered security, and police in Warsaw strengthened measures in crowded tourist areas. The U.K. Foreign Office updated its Poland travel advice following the shooting.

Next, Saudi Arabia. Reuters reports an Aramco building in Riyadh was damaged by falling debris after an attempted attack, according to the ministry. The Institute for the Study of War says the Houthis continued to target Saudi critical infrastructure on Friday and Saturday, and that Saudi Arabia intercepted a Houthi drone and cruise missile launched toward Riyadh on Friday, November sixth.

Turning to Iran and the Strait of Hormuz. The Institute for the Study of War says the United States conducted limited strikes on Iranian air defenses on Qeshm Island on Friday, November sixth, amid continued Iranian drone, missile and limpet mine attacks on commercial vessels. Radio Free Europe reports U.S. Central Command said it fired on and disabled a commercial ship whose crew ignored repeated warnings and tried to violate the naval blockade against Iran. Reuters also reports exclusively that the U.S. military is looking to restrict Iranian land commerce.

In Ukraine. The Institute for the Study of War says Russian forces targeted Kyiv with thirty six ballistic missiles overnight Friday into Saturday as part of their campaign against energy infrastructure. Radio Free Europe reports Russian attacks on the power system in the Dnipropetrovsk region have left six hundred thousand people without electricity, with hospitals running on diesel generators.

And in Guatemala. Reuters reports a Guatemalan lawmaker and three police officers were killed in an armed attack.

Now a few quick notes by region.

In the Middle East and North Africa, Reuters reports Egypt has reopened its embassy in Libya's capital, Tripoli, after a twelve year closure. Radio Free Europe reports Iran executed three men arrested in connection with the January anti government protests in Karaj. And Reuters reports heavy rain flooded tents housing displaced people in Gaza.

In South Asia, Tribal News Network reports, citing the district police chief, that a police constable was killed by an improvised explosive device in Pakistan's Shangla district.

And in cyber, the Italian daily La Repubblica reports the company building the Brenner Base Tunnel between Austria and Italy has confirmed it was hit by a cyberattack.
